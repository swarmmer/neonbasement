You Can See Everything

2026-10-29 · David P. Carrano   •   Tags: [#Personal #FilmReview #YouCanSeeEverything]

The Relationship Chameleon

Have you ever known someone who adopts the musical preferences of whatever person they happen to be dating? Or maybe someone you know who historically hates seafood starts eating sushi after a few dates with a sushi enthusiast. It's not to say these people are total pushovers. Just that attraction and even love can manifest in strange ways. Like a sudden taste for sushi.

I knew a guy like this once. His entire persona would change with every new girl he met. It's not that he didn't have any agency for himself. I actually think he enjoyed being influenced by other people so profoundly. He was never really secure with his own personality. So why not adopt someone else's?

He's dead now. He adopted a few too many bad traits from a few too many bad people. But there is a lesson I learned from his constant personality shifts. You can take things from people, in moderation. I got my entire sense of fashion from Josh Hartnett.

My number one influence has been my wife. She's made me a better person. But there is a line between letting someone shape you and letting them overwrite you entirely. I think we all love a little differently, and for my friend, that total surrender was how he showed it. Letting the person wash over him, consume him, control him.

Some people are just malleable like that.

I think the Holmes couple is a perfect example of this. They lie to themselves, sure, but it goes deeper than that. There’s an unspoken understanding between them that what they are doing is a complete fabrication. They are performing. But they keep that performance up even when the cameras are off, even when it's just the two of them in an empty room. And ultimately, could you ever really prove someone like that is lying?

Near the end of the film, Nathan can be seen casually drinking from a "World's Best Dad" coffee cup. It’s a callback to Holmes’s bizarre comment when first meeting him. It’s a tiny detail, but it says a lot. Nathan, it appears, is a little taken by Holmes. Watching him hold that cup, you see him trying to be someone he is not. He takes a simple comment from Holmes and internalizes it so much. He is so malleable. But maybe that is the real difference between the two of them: Nathan is just empty and searching for an identity to adopt, while Holmes is operating out of pure, sociopathic narcissism.
